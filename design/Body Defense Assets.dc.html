Defenders — six different verbs

None of them is "a gun". Each neutralizes the way the real cell does.
UNLOCK ORDER

THE INTENDED COMBO Block → Tag → everything else
A clot at the mouth stretches the time everything spends in range. An antibody behind it strips armour, starts the burn, and doubles what a mast cell does. Phagocytes cover each other's rest windows; a killer cell mops up whatever they wounded. Every defender is a multiplier on another one — none of them wins a case alone, and that's the lesson each case is teaching.

Case types

A case changes a rule. It never just adds more enemies.
Wound SHIPPED
Bleeds 2 energy/sec until a clot is down
Endless weak bacteria. The clock is the real enemy — you're buying your own economy back.
Virus SHIPPED
Every kill splits into two
Forces you to kill early and in bulk. Rewards stacking defenders at the entrance, not the exit.
Poisoning SHIPPED
Toxins kill your cells, not the tissue
Defenders take damage and die. Antibodies survive it far better — reach beats proximity.
Allergy DESIGNED, NOT BUILT
Nothing is really invading — overreacting damages you
Inverts the whole game. Probably too clever for the first hour; hold it for later in the run.
Chronic DESIGNED, NOT BUILT
Never fully clears — hold it low across days
The only case with no win screen. Occupies a region permanently and taxes the run.

What it teaches
One cell can't hold a lane. Armour needs a tag. Some things can't be tagged at all.
Kill early, kill in bulk. Chip damage loses to anything that heals.
Your board is a target too. Position back from the vessel, not on it.

THE FIVE PACING RULES
01Never a new enemy and a new defender in the same wave. The player must be able to attribute what changed.
02A new enemy arrives while its answer is already in the dock. Biofilm shows up on wave 3 and Tag has been sitting there since wave 1 — the discovery is the player's, not a tutorial's.
03Every new enemy invalidates the current best strategy. If a pathogen doesn't force the board to change shape, it's just a bigger number and it gets cut.
04Rewards are deterministic. Clear case 1, get Burst. No random drops, no chests, no paid shortcuts.
05Waves 1—2 of every case are winnable with what you already know. The ramp lives in waves 3—5, so a short session is never a wasted session.
TWO CURVES, DELIBERATELY OFFSET
Threat
Your tools
Tools step up after the threat does, so each case starts slightly uncomfortable and ends comfortable. The tool curve flattens at case 3 — the last two cases are won with mastery, not new toys.
COPY RULES
Headlines are physical, not clinical. "It got into the blood." not "Defeat". Real names, honest behaviour. If we call it a T-cell it has to behave a bit like one. Never scold the player. A lost region states what happened and offers the next move. No exclamation marks. No emoji. The body is calm even when it's losing.
MOTION RULES
Sheets rise 14px over 250ms. Nothing slides sideways, nothing bounces. Only threats pulse. A pulsing ring always means "here, now" — never decoration. Kills are instant. No death animation; the energy number ticking up is the feedback. The sim pauses when unseen. Backgrounding the game never costs a wave.
OPEN QUESTIONS
Is Allergy too clever for the first hour, or is it the thing people tell their friends about? Should a lost region stay lost for the whole run, or heal after two days? Six defenders is a full dock on a phone. Is a seventh a replacement rather than an addition? Does the heart ever get attacked directly, or is it only ever the thing you're protecting?
